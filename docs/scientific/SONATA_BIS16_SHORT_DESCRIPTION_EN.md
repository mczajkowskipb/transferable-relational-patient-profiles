# From Cohort-Specific Omics Patterns to Transferable Relational Patient Profiles

## 1. Research goal, problem, questions and hypotheses

Omics studies often discover patient groups that remain meaningful only inside the cohort in which they were found. When the laboratory, platform, population composition or preprocessing changes, investigators commonly repeat clustering from the beginning. The new partition may be stable within the new dataset, yet there is no guarantee that it corresponds to the groups discovered previously. This is particularly limiting in biomedical omics, where cohorts are often modest, independent replication is expensive, and a cohort-specific partition is difficult to reuse as a scientific object.

This project asks a different question: **can patient-group structure discovered in one omics cohort be encoded as a compact relational patient profile that remains executable, interpretable and scientifically testable under cohort and platform shift without target-guided retraining - and can we determine when such transfer should be rejected?**

The proposed solution is to learn patient groups directly as sparse executable sets of within-sample feature-order relations, for example `gene A > gene B` or `gene C < gene D`. A relational patient profile (RPP) is therefore not merely an explanation attached after clustering. It is the operational definition of a discovered group. The same frozen object defines membership, provides a human-readable explanation and can be executed on an unseen sample without reclustering the target cohort. If the evidence is insufficient, the framework can return `NO_STABLE_STRUCTURE` at discovery or `UNASSIGNED` at transfer.

The **main objective** is to develop the theoretical and computational foundations for converting cohort-specific omics discoveries into reusable relational patient profiles that can be transferred to independent cohorts and individual samples without target-guided retraining.

The **central hypothesis** is deliberately conditional rather than universal. We hypothesise that stable within-sample feature-order relations can encode patient-group structure in a form that is less sensitive to selected classes of cohort and platform shift than conventional value-space descriptions. When a group is supported by stable relative orderings, it should be possible to learn a sparse executable profile that retains meaningful source-to-target agreement and single-sample executability. For explicitly declared perturbation classes, source relation margins should additionally permit **conditional transportability certificates** and a profile-level **Relational Transportability Radius (RTR)**: a source-calibrated lower bound on how far a frozen assignment can be perturbed before its score/margin conditions cease to be guaranteed. When these assumptions are violated, source-only reliability criteria and target-side feature coverage or assignment margins should trigger abstention rather than forced transfer.

Four linked questions organise the project: **LEARN** - how should transferable relational patient profiles be defined and induced directly? **TRUST** - when is the discovered relational structure identifiable and stable enough to justify a reusable profile? **TRANSFER** - when can a frozen profile be executed in an independent cohort without target-guided retraining? **MAP THE LIMITS** - which distribution shifts preserve, weaken or destroy transferability?

## 2. Significance, state of the art and novelty

The problem is not simply that clustering algorithms disagree. A more fundamental difficulty is that a cluster is usually represented implicitly by its position in a dataset-specific geometry: distances between normalised values, centroids, medoids, embeddings or graph neighbourhoods. Repeating the analysis in another cohort often creates a new geometry and a new partition. Standard stability analysis [2,3] can show that a partition is internally reproducible, but it does not turn that partition into a frozen, reusable definition that can be applied to one future sample.

Within-sample relative orderings offer a complementary representation. Gene-pair classifiers such as Top-Scoring Pairs demonstrated that simple comparisons can support transparent single-sample prediction and reduce dependence on absolute measurement scale [4,5,7]. Relative-expression-ordering approaches have subsequently been used in multiple supervised and N-of-1 settings [5-7]. However, success in supervised classification does not establish an unsupervised theory of reusable patient groups. In clustering there are no outcome labels to guide relation selection, the pair space grows combinatorially, multiple equivalent rule sets may describe the same latent structure, and technical changes can selectively flip individual relations.

Interpretable clustering methods can produce rules or compact explanations, while domain-generalisation and certified-robustness methods address distribution shift mainly for predictive models [9,10,12]. Domain generalisation itself has been analysed as extrapolation beyond observed source distributions [12]. RPP reuse has a related extrapolative direction, but here the object carried beyond the discovery cohort is a **frozen group definition**, not a numerical response. Yet target-domain adaptation changes the model using information from the new domain, whereas the scientific question here is stricter: **can the definition learned from the source be frozen before the target is evaluated?** The project therefore separates discovery from evaluation. Clinical or phenotype labels never define the profiles; they are revealed only after source profiles and target assignments are frozen and serve as external evidence of biological usefulness.

The novelty is not a claim that pairwise relations, clustering, abstention, domain shift or interpretability are individually new. It lies in their joint formulation around a reusable scientific object: a patient group represented as a sparse executable relational profile, learned directly during unsupervised discovery, frozen for single-sample execution, equipped with explicit abstention and conditional source-derived transportability certificates, and evaluated under a strict source/target boundary. The project will additionally determine when alternative relational descriptions are identifiable, when a discovered group should be withheld as `NO_STABLE_STRUCTURE`, and when a transferred sample should remain `UNASSIGNED`.

This formulation changes the expected output of clustering. Instead of publishing only “cluster 1 / cluster 2” for one cohort, a study can in principle publish a versioned profile containing its relations, weights, required features, thresholds, provenance and evidence of external transfer. A later study can test exactly the same definition rather than rediscovering groups from scratch. The project remains basic research: it will establish the mathematical and empirical conditions for such reuse, not claim clinical utility.

## 3. Concept, work plan and preliminary evidence

The 48-month project is organised as **LEARN -> TRUST -> TRANSFER -> MAP THE LIMITS**.

**WP1 - Learning Relational Patient Profiles (M1-12).** We will formalise RPPs as sparse partial-order structures and define profile equivalence, redundancy and identifiability. Direct induction will jointly optimise patient assignments and a bounded set of relations using within-group support, between-group contrast, sparsity, redundancy control and executable coverage. Deterministic tie-breaking and explicit missingness handling will make the learned object reproducible. Conventional value-space clustering, relational-distance clustering and relational clustering followed by post-hoc profile extraction will provide matched baselines.

**WP2 - Knowing When to Trust a Profile (M7-24).** Source-only diagnostics will test whether the apparent structure is sufficiently stable and identifiable to support reuse. We will combine perturbation stability, profile stability, minimum group size, null-calibrated structure strength and relation redundancy. WP2 will also derive a **Relational Transportability Certificate (RTC)** for declared shift classes. The RPP and thresholds will first be frozen on a SOURCE-FIT subset; an independent SOURCE-CALIBRATION subset will then provide pointwise assignment-robustness radii. Exact distribution-free one-sided tolerance bounds based on order statistics [11] will convert these radii into a profile-level **RTR** at predeclared population coverage/confidence. If the calibration sample is too small, the result is `NOT_CERTIFIABLE`. Platform annotation provides a separate metadata-only executability bound. These certificates remain conditional on their stated shift model and will be tested, not assumed, to predict real transfer. Failure to establish reliable structure will return `NO_STABLE_STRUCTURE`; it will not trigger target-guided recalibration.

**WP3 - Transferring Profiles across Cohorts (M18-38).** A complete source artifact - feature universe, preprocessing, relations, weights, cluster number, thresholds and rejection rules - will be frozen before target outcomes are opened. The primary prospective lung module is frozen as **GSE19804 -> GSE27262 and GSE32863**. One unchanged source artifact must be executed in both targets. The primary gate requires executable coverage >=0.70 and forced all-sample ARI >=0.50 in both targets; a failed target cannot be replaced after unsealing. A secondary colorectal module is frozen at accession level as **GSE39582 -> GSE14333 and GSE33113**; its exact phenotype endpoint will be finalised only after a sample-level metadata audit and before outcome labels are opened. Target cohorts cannot select features, relations, K, thresholds or mapping rules.

**WP4 - Mapping the Boundaries of Transferability (M31-48).** Controlled shifts will map where relational profiles remain useful, become neutral or fail. Stress tests will include global scale changes, gene-specific shifts, missing features, platform overlap, nuisance structure, unequal mixtures and changes in cluster prevalence. The final result will be a domain-of-applicability map linking source-observable diagnostics and target-side executability to successful transfer or justified abstention. A machine-readable, registry-ready profile specification will capture the scientific object required for independent reuse.

Preliminary work supports feasibility but also exposes the exact failure modes that motivate the project. In a frozen representation-audit pilot covering 630 controlled source-target pairs, the source-only procedure identified the generating representation family in 93.3% of signal replicates, with median target ARI regret 0 and a 6.7% false-structure rate under NULL; nevertheless, its preregistered external-transfer Gate C remained **STOP**. The subsequent direct relational-profile pilot (`RR_DIRECT`) showed median ARI 1.0 in synthetic relational regimes and promising frozen bidirectional transfer between GSE10072 and GSE19804: ARI 0.771 at 96.7% coverage in one direction and ARI 0.960 at 92.5% coverage in the other. However, the prospective pilot gate again remained **STOP (4/5 criteria passed)** because exact recovery of designated pairs failed in a non-identifiable generator. A diagnostic generator that separated the order blocks, with unchanged hyperparameters, then produced exact pair recovery and source/target ARI 1.0 across 120 replicates. On eleven real datasets, behaviour was heterogeneous and median RR_DIRECT ARI was low, so the project explicitly does **not** assume broad superiority. These negative and mixed results are retained as evidence that identifiability, abstention and applicability boundaries are central scientific questions rather than afterthoughts.

## 4. Methodology and evaluation

All primary experiments will enforce a strict label and target-information firewall. Measurement matrices and evaluation labels will be separated at the data-interface level. Source fitting will produce a serialised artifact with ordered feature identifiers, mapping rules, selected relations, weights, thresholds, software versions and hashes. Target expression values may only execute this artifact. Target labels cannot alter source preprocessing, relation selection, cluster number or rejection thresholds.

For an RPP P_k = {r_k1,...,r_km}, a sample score will be based on the weighted fraction of executable relations that are satisfied. Assignment requires both sufficient executable coverage and a source-calibrated margin over alternative profiles; otherwise the sample is `UNASSIGNED`. Profiles will be sparse by design. Redundant inverse relations, excessive reuse of one feature and relation sets that are statistically indistinguishable under the source data will be controlled explicitly.

The controlled benchmark will separate at least four classes of shift: (i) transformations expected to preserve within-sample order, (ii) feature-specific transformations that can invert selected relations, (iii) missing-feature/platform-overlap shifts, and (iv) mixture or nuisance shifts that alter apparent group structure. This makes the main hypothesis falsifiable: relational profiles should not be expected to survive every form of distribution shift.

Primary source-side outcomes will include profile stability, bootstrap relation Jaccard, group non-degeneracy, identifiability/equivalence diagnostics, false-structure behaviour under matched nulls, and calibration of RTC certified-coverage curves under controlled perturbations. Target-side outcomes will include executable coverage, `UNASSIGNED` rate, forced all-sample ARI/NMI, assigned-sample agreement as a secondary measure, profile-score margins, relation flip rates and retained external biological associations. Forced all-sample metrics are important because selective rejection must not artificially inflate the primary quality estimate.

Comparisons will use matched value-space and relational clustering baselines under the same feature budgets and evaluation splits. `RR_POSTHOC` will distinguish the value of a direct profile-learning objective from simply explaining a conventional relational partition after the fact. Conventional cluster-then-classify procedures and supervised phenotype classifiers may be used only as secondary comparators; supervised prediction is not a co-equal project aim.

Uncertainty will be estimated by patient-level resampling, preserving paired or repeated specimens. All preregistered directions and cohorts will be reported, including failures. No failed external target can be replaced, and no post-label optimisation can alter a primary result. This design makes negative findings scientifically informative: they define the boundary at which a reusable profile ceases to be justified.

## 5. Team, feasibility, risks and expected impact

The project is designed as a new methodological programme rather than a continuation of supervised classification. The PI will lead formalisation, algorithm design, experimental architecture and synthesis of the transferability theory. The PI has previously led completed NCN PRELUDIUM and OPUS projects and has developed relational and interpretable machine-learning methods for biomedical data; the recently accepted taxonomic review of rank-based relational methods provides a direct conceptual foundation for the proposed programme.

A doctoral researcher recruited for at least 36 months will develop direct profile induction, identifiability diagnostics and stability analysis as a coherent dissertation topic. A postdoctoral researcher recruited in open competition will lead independent cohort curation, platform mapping, cross-cohort evaluation and reproducibility review. This new-team structure is intentional: it separates construction of the methodological object from the data harmonisation and transfer-validation layer and creates a distinct research line around unsupervised relational profiling.

The main risks are explicit. **No stable structure:** the method returns `NO_STABLE_STRUCTURE`. **Non-identifiable rule sets:** equivalence classes or stable partial orders are reported instead of claiming exact recovery. **Direct induction adds no value over post-hoc explanation:** the direct method is not promoted as superior. **Low cross-platform feature coverage:** samples remain `UNASSIGNED`, and the failure becomes part of the applicability map. **Stable but biologically irrelevant groups:** structural stability and phenotype association are reported separately. **A favourable result depends on one cohort or direction:** prospective multi-target freezes and complete reporting prevent replacement of failed tests.

If successful, the project will establish a principled route from a cohort-specific omics discovery to a reusable, falsifiable and conditionally certifiable patient-group definition. The scientific outputs will be: a formal model of relational patient profiles; deterministic direct-induction algorithms; source-only trust and abstention criteria; prospective evidence for or against frozen cross-cohort transfer; and an empirical map of the distribution shifts under which reuse remains justified. The resulting profile specification will be versionable and machine-readable and can be executed locally on one molecular sample without reclustering a target cohort. This naturally supports future distributed reuse without requiring central pooling of raw target molecular matrices, although the project does not claim formal privacy guarantees or federated learning.
